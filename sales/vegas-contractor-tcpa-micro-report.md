# State of Vegas Contractor TCPA Risk

**A 2026 briefing for wholesale brokers and the trades they place.**

## The one-line version

TCPA lawsuits nationally are up 60% year over year, and the trades — HVAC, plumbing, roofing,
solar, home services — are disproportionately named because they run high-volume SMS dispatch
and appointment-reminder campaigns with the least documentation to defend them. A Las Vegas
contractor running AI-assisted SMS dispatch or an AI receptionist today has real, uncapped,
per-message exposure and almost never has a signed audit trail proving otherwise.

## Why now

- **2,628 TCPA lawsuits were filed nationally in 2025, up 60% over 2024** (CompliancePoint, *2025
  TCPA Litigation Trends*). A separate tracking methodology counted 1,807 TCPA class actions in
  2025 vs. 915 in 2024 — a 97% increase — with September 2025 alone setting a single-month record
  of 224 class actions, up 283% over September 2024 (National Law Review / TCPAWorld). The two
  counts differ by methodology (all suits vs. class actions only), but they agree on direction and
  magnitude: this is accelerating, not leveling off.
- **Nearly 80% of TCPA suits are filed as class actions**, versus 2–5% for comparable consumer
  protection statutes (National Law Review) — meaning a single bad campaign doesn't produce one
  claim, it produces a class.
- **Statutory damages run $500–$1,500 per message, with no cap.** A contractor running a
  2,000-message appointment-reminder campaign is looking at seven-figure exposure from one
  campaign, not from a pattern of abuse.
- **AI adoption in the trades is accelerating faster than the compliance layer underneath it.**
  AI receptionists and AI-assisted SMS dispatch are now common in HVAC/plumbing/roofing shops
  chasing speed-to-lead — but the TCPA treats an AI voice agent as an "artificial voice," and
  scripted disclosure/consent gaps that were low-stakes with a human dispatcher become
  systematic, repeatable liability at AI scale.

## Where the exposure actually sits (not where contractors think it does)

Three gaps show up over and over in Grandeza's compliance scans of contractor intake systems —
and none of them are about whether the contractor "does spam texting." They're about
documentation that was never built:

1. **Consent provenance, not just consent.** A contractor who buys or inherits a lead list (from
   a marketing vendor, a franchise parent, or an acquired book of business) is texting numbers
   with no consent captured *for that contractor specifically*. Consent does not transfer with a
   list. This is the single most common gap and the easiest for a plaintiff's firm to prove.
2. **No opt-out or quiet-hours enforcement at the system level.** Dispatch and reminder texts
   sent outside permitted contact windows, or to numbers that opted out days or weeks earlier
   through a channel the dispatch system doesn't check, generate exposure the contractor doesn't
   know exists until a demand letter arrives.
3. **No audit trail.** Even contractors doing consent and timing right usually can't *prove* it —
   no timestamped, tamper-evident record of when consent was captured, what was said, and when an
   opt-out was honored. In litigation, "we're pretty sure we did this right" and "here's the
   signed, timestamped record" are different outcomes.

## What this means for a wholesale broker's book

Every contractor a broker places into E&O/GL or a specialty program is carrying this exposure
whether or not it's ever been named in a claim. A one-time TCPA loss on an uninsured or
underinsured contractor is a claims event that follows the broker's placement, not just the
contractor. Grandeza's free scan (`grandeza.io/scan`) gives a contractor — or a broker doing due
