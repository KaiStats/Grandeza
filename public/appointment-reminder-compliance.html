SMS appointment reminder compliance

Reminder texts feel harmless — which is exactly why they're the most common message shops send without a defensible record. Here's how to do them right.

Last updated: July 2026 · ~5 min read · Educational, not legal advice.

"Transactional" still has rules

An appointment reminder for a job the customer booked is a transactional message, which carries a lighter consent standard than marketing. But "lighter" isn't "none": you still need to show the customer gave you that number to be contacted about their service, and you must let them opt out. Skip either and the reminder is a liability.

The fastest way to turn a safe reminder into a risky one is to bolt a promotion onto it.

Keep service and marketing separate

Adding "and 15% off a tune-up!" to a reminder can reclassify the whole message as marketing — which requires prior express written consent. Keep transactional reminders clean, and run promotions as their own, separately-consented stream.

A compliant reminder checklist

  • Consent on file — a record that the customer provided the number for service updates.
  • Clear opt-out — honor STOP instantly and log it.
  • Reasonable timing & frequency — daytime hours, and don't stack multiple reminders from different systems (rate limits help).
  • DNC honored — respect internal opt-outs even for service messages (more on DNC).
  • Logged — every reminder in a searchable audit trail, so you can prove what you sent and why.
