Security

We hold ourselves to the standard we sell.

Grandeza handles consent records, call and message logs, and the audit trail our clients rely on in a dispute. That data is evidence. This page documents how we protect it — encryption, audit cadence, penetration testing, incident response, and how to tell us if we've got something wrong.

Every claim below carries its current status. We would rather show you a roadmap you can check than a programme you have to take on trust — and a security page that overstates itself is exactly the failure our own product exists to find in other people.

Status verified: 3 August 2026 · Reviewed quarterly

01 · Data protection

Encrypted at rest. Encrypted in transit. No exceptions.

At rest

We hold no customer dataLive

The strongest control is not encrypting data well — it is not holding it. Grandeza operates no database. The scan runs entirely in your browser: your answers and your score never leave the device, and there is no server-side store of client data to encrypt, breach or subpoena. Open your developer tools and watch the network tab while you take it. Any data we hold in future will be encrypted at rest with AES-256, and this page will say so on the day it is true.

In transit

TLS 1.2+ everywhere, HSTS enforcedLive

All traffic to and from Grandeza is served over TLS 1.2 or higher. HTTP is redirected, never served. HSTS is set with max-age=31536000 and includeSubDomains, so once a browser has seen this site it refuses an unencrypted connection for a year. We have not submitted to the browser preload list; that is a one-way door and we will not claim it until it is done.

Key management

Managed keys, rotation in handIn progress

No key material lives in our code or configuration — verified by automated scanning on every commit. The keys that exist today are our DKIM mail-signing keys, held by our email provider. Those are currently 1024-bit and are being reissued at 2048-bit; until that lands this reads in progress rather than done. A fixed rotation schedule follows.

Access

Least privilege, MFA, loggedIn progress

Production reach means two accounts: the code repository and the hosting platform. Both are being brought under mandatory multi-factor authentication, and both already log every action to their own audit trail. Formal quarterly access review is not yet a scheduled process, which is why this reads in progress. With no application logins to manage, the access surface is small enough to state precisely rather than describe in the abstract.

02 · Production audit

Thirteen layers.In progress

The same discipline we run against a client's intake, run against our own stack. The first full pass completed in August 2026 and its findings are fixed; twelve of the checks now run automatically on every commit. Making the whole thirteen a per-release gate is the remaining work, which is why this reads in progress.

01

Network perimeter

Edge firewall rules, DDoS mitigation and rate limiting reviewed against the current threat profile.

02

Transport security

TLS configuration, cipher suites, certificate validity and HSTS enforcement verified end to end.

03

Authentication

Credential handling, MFA enforcement, session lifetime and lockout behaviour tested against bypass attempts.

04

Authorization

Role boundaries and object-level permissions checked for horizontal and vertical privilege escalation.

05

Input handling

Injection, deserialization and traversal surfaces exercised across every request path that accepts user input.

06

Output encoding

Cross-site scripting and content-injection vectors validated, with Content Security Policy enforced in report-and-block mode.

07

Data at rest

Encryption coverage confirmed across primary stores, replicas, backups and snapshots — no unencrypted copies.

08

Secrets management

Repositories, images and configuration scanned for committed credentials, tokens and private keys.

09

Dependencies

Full dependency tree resolved against known-vulnerability databases, with a defined remediation window by severity.

10

Logging & audit trail

Security-relevant events confirmed to be captured, tamper-evident, and retained for the contracted period.

11

Backup & recovery

Restores tested from real backups — not assumed. Recovery time and recovery point measured against target.

12

Third-party surface

Every sub-processor and integration reviewed for the data it receives and the access it holds.

13

Configuration drift

Running production compared against declared infrastructure state; any unreviewed divergence is treated as a finding.

03 · Penetration testing

Twice a year on the calendar, and again on every material change.Planned

A once-a-year test tells you the system was sound on one day twelve months ago. We commission an independent full-scope penetration test twice a year, and we don't wait for the next window: any material change to authentication, data storage or the intake pipeline is retested before it ships.

Semi-annual

Independent third-party test, twice a yearPlanned

Our commitment is a full-scope test by an independent security firm twice a year, above the annual baseline SOC 2 and ISO 27001 expect. The first engagement is not yet booked, so this is a commitment rather than a record — we will publish the date of the first completed test here, and offer clients a summary letter under NDA from that point.

Event-driven

Retest after material changePlanned

Any material change to authentication, data storage or the intake pipeline will trigger a targeted retest of the affected surface before it reaches production, rather than waiting for the next scheduled window. New weakness arrives with new code, not with the calendar. This begins with the first engagement above.

Continuous

Automated checks on every buildLive

Twelve automated checks run on every commit today: secret and private-key detection, content-security-policy validation against every external origin, integrity fingerprinting of the scan application, link and sitemap resolution, and cache-policy coverage. Each exists because that specific thing broke in production at least once. This runs continuously between scheduled tests — not as a substitute for them.

Remediation windows

Fixed by severity, not by moodPlanned

The targets are 7 days for critical, 30 for high, 90 for medium, with anything outside the window escalated as a documented accepted risk. A tracker enforcing those windows is not yet in place, which is why this reads planned — today findings are tracked in our issue backlog without an enforced clock.

Clients under an active engagement may request a summary letter of the most recent penetration test under NDA. We do not publish raw findings, as they describe live systems.

04 · Incident response

We maintain a written incident response plan.Live

The plan is documented, assigns named roles, and is rehearsed — so the first time we run it is not during a real incident.

Step 01

Detect & declare

Monitoring and alerting escalate to an on-call responder. Any responder can declare an incident; nobody has to seek permission first.

Step 02

Contain

Containment takes priority over root cause. Credentials are rotated, affected access revoked, and the blast radius bounded before investigation continues.

Step 03

Assess & notify

Scope and data exposure are established. Affected clients are notified without undue delay, with regulatory notification timelines treated as a ceiling, not a target.

Step 04

Eradicate & recover

Root cause is removed and service restored from a known-good state, with integrity verified before traffic returns.

Step 05

Post-incident review

A blameless review is completed within 10 business days, producing corrective actions with named owners and due dates.

Ongoing

Tabletop exercise

The plan is written and the runbooks are specific to this stack. The first tabletop rehearsal is scheduled but has not yet run, so the plan is documented rather than proven. Gaps found in rehearsal will be tracked like production findings.

05 · Coordinated disclosure

Found something? Tell us.

We would rather hear it from you than from a claimant. If you believe you have found a vulnerability in a Grandeza system, report it and we will work it with you.

Report to

security@grandeza.io

Use this address for security reports only. For privacy requests use privacy@grandeza.io; for anything else, hello@grandeza.io.

Report a vulnerability →

What to include

  • The affected asset — domain, endpoint or feature.
  • Clear reproduction steps, and a proof of concept if you have one.
  • The impact you believe it has, and any prerequisites an attacker would need.
  • How you would like to be credited, if the finding is confirmed.

What we commit to

  • Acknowledgement within 1 business day that a human has your report.
  • Triage assessment within 5 business days, including whether we accept the finding.
  • Progress updates until the issue is closed, and credit on request once it is fixed.
  • No legal action against good-faith research that follows the rules below.

Safe harbour & ground rules

We will not pursue legal action against researchers who act in good faith and stay inside these boundaries. Testing that breaks them falls outside safe harbour.

  • Do not access, modify or exfiltrate data that is not yours. If you encounter client data, stop and tell us immediately.
  • Do not degrade service — no denial-of-service, no load or stress testing, no automated scanning that generates disruptive volume.
  • Do not use social engineering, physical intrusion, or attacks against our staff, vendors or offices.
  • Give us reasonable time to fix before any public disclosure, and coordinate the timing with us.
  • Stay within our own systems. Third-party services we use are out of scope; report those to their owners.

Grandeza does not currently operate a paid bug bounty. We do credit confirmed reporters, with their permission, once a fix has shipped.
