This is settled law, not speculation

The regulator already decided
that AI voice is covered.

AI voice = “artificial voice”

The FCC ruled that AI-generated and cloned voices are “artificial” under the TCPA, with no carve-out for technology designed to sound like a live agent. Consent, identification and opt-out obligations all attach.

FCC Declaratory Ruling · Feb 2024

Opt-out is live law today

The FCC pushed the “revoke-all” rule to 31 Jan 2027 — but every other revocation duty is in force now. A system that keeps messaging after STOP is an open-and-shut violation.

FCC CGB Order DA-26-12 · Jan 2026

Liability follows the seller

In the Farmers matter the calls and texts came from an agency selling the product. The company whose product was being sold still paid. Contractors sit in the same structural seat.

Starling settlement · publicized late Jun 2026
Sources: FCC Declaratory Ruling on AI-generated voice, 8 Feb 2024 · FCC Consumer & Governmental Affairs Bureau Order DA-26-12, 6 Jan 2026 · Starling v. Farmers Ins. Exch., No. 26SL-CC00138.

The published compliance posture
“Our automated calling service integrates with DNC registries and respects your uploaded suppression lists.”

The complete published TCPA position on the outbound page. No consent capture, no calling-window rules, no AI disclosure, no opt-out architecture.

What the contract does say

§6.2 makes the customer solely responsible for complying with “any Laws”; §6.4 requires them to indemnify Newo. The exposure is already routed to the contractor.

None of this is a knock on Newo — it is where the whole category sits. The ISO 27001, HIPAA and GDPR work already done proves the appetite for regulated procurement; those frameworks govern how data is stored, not whether a number consented to be dialed.
